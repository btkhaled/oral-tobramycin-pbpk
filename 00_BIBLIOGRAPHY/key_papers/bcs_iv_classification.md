# BCS Classification — Literature Analysis

## 1. Amidon et al. 1995 — Foundation

### Citation
Amidon GL, Lennernäs H, Shah VP, Crison JR. A theoretical basis for a biopharmaceutic drug classification: the correlation of in vitro drug product dissolution and in vivo bioavailability. Pharm Res. 1995;12(3):413-420.

### Key Points
- Defined 4 BCS classes based on solubility and permeability
- **Class I**: High solubility, High permeability (e.g., metoprolol)
- **Class II**: Low solubility, High permeability (e.g., diclofenac)
- **Class III**: High solubility, Low permeability (e.g., ranitidine)
- **Class IV**: Low solubility, Low permeability (e.g., furosemide)
- Solubility: highest dose in ≤250 mL over pH 1-6.8
- Permeability: compared to IV reference

### Implications for Tobramycin
- Solubility is clearly HIGH (94 mg/mL)
- Permeability is clearly LOW (polycationic)
- **Classification: BCS III** (not IV)

## 2. FDA M9 Guidance (2021)

### Key Points
- BCS-based biowaivers apply to Class I and III
- Class III: high solubility + low permeability
- Eligibility: immediate-release, oral, systemic action
- Same dosage form and strength as reference
- Excipient effects must be addressed

### Implications
- BCS III drugs can potentially qualify for biowaivers
- Regulatory pathway exists for oral tobramycin

---

## 3. ICH M9 (2019)

### Key Points
- International harmonized guidance
- Same classification as FDA
- Same biowaiver criteria
- Supports global regulatory strategy

## 4. Dahan et al. 2021 — Provisional BCS Classification

### Key Points
- Provisional classification of world's top oral drugs
- Many drugs reclassified with updated data
- Regional absorption differences considered

---

## 5. Wu & Benet 2005 — BDDCS

### Key Points
- BDDCS = Biopharmaceutics Drug Disposition Classification System
- Extends BCS to predict drug disposition
- Class III BCS = Class III BDDCS
- Relates to transporter effects

---

## 6. Debate: BCS III vs IV for Tobramycin

### Arguments for BCS III
1. Solubility clearly high (94 mg/mL)
2. Asad et al. 2023 explicitly state "BCS class-III"
3. Multiple references confirm high solubility
4. Problem is permeability, not solubility

### Arguments for BCS IV (Potential)
1. Some formulations may show low apparent solubility
2. Polycationic nature may affect dissolution in some media
3. Complex salt forms may have different behavior
4. Very low oral F (~1-2%) suggests severe limitation

### Resolution
- Native tobramycin = BCS III
- Certain formulations (complexes, salts) may behave as IV
- Study should address both classifications
- Focus on permeability enhancement regardless
